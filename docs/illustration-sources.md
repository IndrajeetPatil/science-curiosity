# Illustration sources

The book has ten illustrations, each with a named creator, source, and
licence. Five come from the original 2011 collection and are credited to an
external source because the image itself matches that source; subject
similarity alone is not enough. The other five are openly licensed Wikimedia
Commons images. The per-image record in
[`figures.json`](../figures.json) contains captions, credits, source links,
verification status, and evidence; the PDF includes the reader-facing
acknowledgements.

| Figure | Subject | Source and licence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dog and mirror | Exact Georgia Pinaud photograph on Wikimedia Commons; public domain. |
| 2 | Buckyballs | Exact NASA/JPL-Caltech artwork PIA13551, cropped. |
| 3 | Antibodies | Exact diagram on Science Aid by Stephen Claydon (2008); educational use with citation. |
| 4 | Intel 80486 die | Thomy pc on Wikimedia Commons; public domain. |
| 5 | Turing test | Exact *Turing Test version 3* by Bilby; public domain. |
| 6 | fMRI scan | John Graner, Walter Reed National Military Medical Center, on Wikimedia Commons; public domain (US government work). |
| 7 | Water-screw machine | Exact Böckler engraving on Wikimedia Commons; public domain. |
| 8 | Snake eating a mouse (thermogram) | Arno / Coen on Wikimedia Commons; CC BY-SA 3.0. |
| 9 | Cloaking simulation | Physicsch on Wikimedia Commons; CC BY 3.0, rendered from SVG. |
| 10 | Axolotl | Vassil on Wikimedia Commons; CC0. |

Each image keeps its own licence terms, and the book's reuse terms do not
extend to them; figure 3 is limited to educational use with citation. Font
licences are included in `fonts/`.
